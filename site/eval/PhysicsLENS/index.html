Official definitions from the PhysicsLENS paper. The benchmark is organised into seven physical domains (collision, gravity, momentum, friction, deformation, fluid, causality), which the paper names but does not define separately; annotators used the eight labels below, which add Contact.

Form fields

Physical plausibility (P), 1–4, all clips
  1. clearly breaks physics, including clipping, floating, objects appearing or disappearing, or impossible motion.
  2. mostly implausible, with one clear issue or several smaller issues.
  3. mostly plausible, with minor oddities only.
  4. physically realistic.

The physical-plausibility score P∈{1,2,3,4} assesses only the visible consistency of contact, motion, deformation, and object persistence. It is a human judgment rather than a measurement against a physical simulator. Task incompletion alone does not imply physical implausibility.

Action completion (A), yes/no, all clips

Judge whether the requested task is completed by the end of the clip, even if the process is physically unrealistic.

The action-completion score A∈{0,1} assesses the final task outcome rather than the physical correctness of the process. A clip may therefore receive A=1 while exhibiting severe physical violations, or A=0 despite otherwise plausible motion.

Hidden-property adherence (H), 1–4, unobservable clips
  1. property completely ignored; the outcome resembles a generic response.
  2. mostly ignored, with only a faint or inconsistent indication.
  3. mostly followed, but with incorrect magnitude, timing, or degree.
  4. fully and correctly reflected.

It is not collected for observable scenarios, and missing values in those scenarios are not interpreted as zero or as failures. Importantly, a low adherence score does not distinguish between two cases: the video may visibly contradict the stated property, or it may fail to perform an interaction that would reveal the property.

Issue description, clips with P<4

Briefly describe what is physically incorrect. Leave blank when P=4.

Violation labels, multi-select, clips with P<4

Select at least one applicable violation category, choosing the most obvious issue first. Leave blank when P=4.

Violation labels

Causality

An effect occurs without a visible cause, or events occur in an impossible order.

Collision

Objects pass through one another or fail to exhibit an expected response after impact.

Contact

An object floats without support, rests at an incorrect contact point, or fails to maintain visible contact.

Deformation

A rigid object warps or melts, or a soft object fails to deform under an interaction that should alter its shape.

Fluid

Pouring, splashing, containment, or other liquid behavior is physically inconsistent.

Friction

Sliding, sticking, or gripping behavior is inconsistent with the visible interaction or stated surface condition.

Gravity

An object floats, falls incorrectly, or otherwise fails to respond plausibly to gravity.

Momentum

An object accelerates or changes motion without a supporting interaction, including apparent creation or loss of energy.

These eight human labels are distinct from the seven physical domains used to organize the benchmark and the tool’s specialists (Section 3.4). In particular, the human codebook includes both Contact and Collision, whereas the domain taxonomy has a single collision family. We preserve the human labels as recorded and merge Contact into Collision in all analyses.

A blank category field indicates only that no label was recorded; it is not independent confirmation that the clip is physically correct.

Some violations may reasonably receive more than one label. For example, an object passing through a supporting surface may involve both Contact and Collision, while unsupported floating may involve both Contact and Gravity.

Annotations were produced by members of the research team, mainly graduate students in computer science, rather than by externally recruited participants. Clips were assigned to annotators at random.
